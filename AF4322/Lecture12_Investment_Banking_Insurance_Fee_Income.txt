=== INTRODUCTION ===
- Financial institutions face struggle to attract funds for loans/investments
- When deposit growth slows, managers pursue new sources of funds and revenue
- IMPORTANT GROWTH SOURCE: FEE INCOME

Fee Income = revenues from charging customers for specific services:
- Monthly service charges on transaction accounts
- Insurance commissions (homes, businesses)
- Membership fees for credit/debit cards
- Financial advisory fees
- "Swipe fees" at point of sale (POS)


=== DRIVERS OF THE FEE INCOME PUSH ===

1. Desire to supplement traditional sources of funds (deposits) when inadequate
2. Lower production costs by offering multiple services using same facilities/resources
   (ECONOMIES OF SCOPE)
3. Offset higher production costs by passing more cost to customers (old AND new services)
4. Reduce overall risk by finding new revenue streams not highly correlated with traditional ones
5. Promote CROSS-SELLING of traditional and new services to enhance revenue and net income

=== EQUITY-LINKED INVESTMENTS (ELI) ===

Definition:
- Products combining notes/deposits with stock options
- Allow bull, bear, or strangle (trading range) bet
- Return based on performance of single equity, basket, or index

Forms:
- Equity-Linked Notes (ELN)
- Equity-Linked Deposits (ELD)
- Equity-Linked Contracts (ELC)
(Per SFC 2003)

ELI Mechanics — Knock-In Triggered (Daily Knock-In Observation):
If closing price is lower than exercise price on expiry AND knock-in is triggered,
investor receives:
(a) PHYSICAL SETTLEMENT
   - Delivery of worst-performing reference asset (fractional share = cash)
   - Potential cash dividend amount
OR
(b) CASH SETTLEMENT
   - Cash equivalent of physical settlement amount
   - Potential cash dividend amount

WORST-CASE SCENARIO:
- Closing price of settlement stock drops to ZERO
- Investor could lose 100% of initial investment


=== SFC PROTECTION FOR ELI INVESTORS ===

1. KNOW YOUR CUSTOMER:
   Licensed/registered persons must establish client's financial situation, investment
   experience, and objectives to assess ability to bear financial risks/losses

2. GIVE REASONABLE ADVICE:
   When recommending ELI, ensure clients are informed of and fully understand
   financial risks and potential losses

3. PROVIDE RISK DISCLOSURE STATEMENT:
   Provide pertinent information regarding ELI; term sheets and risk disclosure
   in addition to minimum content requirements in Code of Conduct

4. DECLARATION BY STAFF AND CONFIRMATION BY CLIENT:
   Sign and date a declaration confirming risk disclosure statement provided in client's
   chosen language; client invited to read, ask questions, and take independent advice

=== KEY INVESTMENT BANKING SERVICES ===

SECURITY UNDERWRITING:
- Best-known and often most profitable IB service
- Purchase for resale of new stocks, bonds, and other instruments in money/capital markets
  on behalf of clients raising new money
- Initial Public Offerings (IPOs) — among most profitable underwriting services

LEVERAGED BUYOUTS (LBOs):
- Acquisition of company, usually by small group of investors
- Typically funded by large amounts of debt

HEDGE FUND BUSINESS:
- Many investment banks recently jumped into this business

Example client questions investment bankers help answer:
- Should we raise new capital? How much, where, how?
- Should we enter new markets at home/abroad? How?
- Should we acquire/merge with other firms? Which? How? When?
- Should we sell our company? What is it worth? How do we find the right buyer?


=== INVESTMENT BANKING POST GRAMM-LEACH-BLILEY (1999) ===

- GLB Act opened full range of IB services for adequately capitalized,
  well-managed commercial banking firms
- Research suggests IB revenue/profitability are positively (not highly) correlated with
  commercial banking — significant product-line diversification effects possible
- Not yet clear that benefits from this new service dimension have offset costs/risks

Future:
- IBs wrestling with what kind of firm to be — what mix achieves high, sustained profitability?
- Some commercial bank-IB combinations show promise despite ongoing post-2007-2009 mortgage losses
- Both IB and commercial banks under intense pressure to raise large new capital
- Many observers anticipate more mergers
- Future bank-IB combinations may not consistently turn out well
- Likely outcome: greater government regulation


=== SELLING INVESTMENT PRODUCTS TO CONSUMERS ===

Why depositors moved funds OUT of bank deposits to investment products:
1. LOW INTEREST RATES: minimal returns on traditional deposits
2. INFLATION CONCERNS: rates often below inflation rate
3. DESIRE FOR HIGHER RETURNS: greater potential, with greater risk
4. DIVERSIFICATION: spreading risk across stocks, bonds, securities + deposits
5. ACCESS TO INVESTMENT MARKETS: tech advancements, online platforms
6. LONG-TERM GOALS: e.g., retirement planning needs growth-potential assets
7. FINANCIAL ADVISOR GUIDANCE: advisors recommend tailored mix of investments


=== MUTUAL FUND INVESTMENT PRODUCTS ===

Definition:
- One of the most popular investment products
- Each share = pro rata claim to dividends/income generated by pool of stocks, bonds, securities
- If liquidated, each investor receives portion of NAV (Net Asset Value) after liabilities paid,
  based on shares held
- Mature, well-established investment vehicle
- Managed by Portfolio Managers
- Pool capital from different investors → invest in various asset classes
  (equities, bonds, money market instruments)
- Holdings could cover dozens of securities

Proprietary funds vs Non-proprietary funds.


=== ADVANTAGES OF MUTUAL FUNDS ===

1. EASE OF ACCESS:
   After establishing investment goals and risk tolerance, retail investors can
   open an account and choose suitable funds.

2. LOWER ENTRY THRESHOLD:
   Accessible to wide variety of investors. In addition to one-off subscription,
   monthly subscription at small amount (e.g., HK$1,000) — optimal way to gain
   global market exposure.

3. LOWER CONCENTRATION RISK:
   Generally invest in many securities. Individual investors can diversify globally
   at relatively lower cost vs holding a single security.


=== TRUST SERVICES AS A SOURCE OF FEE INCOME ===

DEFINITIONS (HKMA Supervisory Policy Manual):

"Trust" — an obligation imposed on a person to hold or control and administer assets
for the benefit of others (beneficiaries) or for a specified purpose
(e.g., charitable purpose, wills, estate planning).

"Trust business" — provision of one or more of the following services, by way of business:
a. Setting up a trust
b. Acting as a trustee (or party performing trustee functions) for a trust
c. Managing assets held on trust
d. Administration services for a trust
e. Eventual transfer of assets to beneficiaries


=== WHAT IS A TRUST? ===
- Created when a TRUST DEED (written document) signed by both Settlor and Trustee
- Trust Deed sets out terms/conditions for assigning Trust Assets to Trustee
- Trustee administers Trust Assets and distributes to Beneficiaries per Trust Deed
- Reflects objectives and intentions laid down by Settlor


=== TRUST SERVICES OVERVIEW ===

- Management of customer property: securities, land, buildings, other assets
- Among the OLDEST non-deposit services banks (and competitors) offer
- Trust departments often generate large deposits (managing customer property)
- Deposits placed by trust department must be FULLY SECURED

Popular kinds of trusts:
- Living trusts
- Testamentary trusts
- Irrevocable trusts
- Charitable trusts
- Indenture trusts

Establishment of FIDUCIARY relationship is critical.


=== HKMA CODE OF PRACTICE FOR TRUST BUSINESS ===

Principle 1: Fairness, honesty and integrity
Trustee should act honestly, fairly, and with integrity in conducting trust business.

Principle 2: Due skill, care and diligence
Act with due skill, care and diligence in interests of customers. Ensure entity and
all relevant staff are fit and proper to perform their roles.

Principle 3: Management and control of trust assets
Exercise due care in understanding, managing, and controlling all trust assets in
full conformity with fiduciary obligations.

Principle 4: Corporate governance and internal controls
Establish proper corporate governance structure; implement adequate internal controls
and risk management systems.

Principle 5: Compliance with legal and regulatory requirements and standards
Comply with all relevant requirements applicable to trust business activities.

Principle 6: Co-operation with regulators
Deal with relevant regulators in an open and co-operative manner.

=== INSURANCE AGENTS vs INSURANCE BROKERS ===

| Aspect | Insurance Broker | Insurance Agent |
|--------|------------------|-----------------|
| Representation | Independent intermediary representing BUYER's interests; not tied to specific insurer | Works for an insurance company; represents INSURER's interests |
| Product Range | Wide range from various insurers; broader selection | Limited to products of employing company |
| Objective | Client-centric; finds best solutions; personalized advice | Company-centric; meets sales targets; promotes employer's policies |
| Compensation | Commission from insurance company when policy placed; varies by insurer/policy | Salaried OR fixed commission from employer; performance-based incentives possible |
| Licensing/Regulation | Subject to licensing/regulatory requirements; must disclose conflicts of interest | Subject to licensing; follow ethical/legal guidelines from employer |


=== MANDATORY DISCLOSURES FOR DEPOSITORY INSTITUTIONS SELLING INSURANCE ===

1. Insurance product or annuity is NOT a deposit or other obligation of depository institution
2. Not insured by FDIC, any other US government agency, the institution itself, or affiliates
3. May involve INVESTMENT RISK and possible loss of value
4. US depository institutions cannot base granting loans on customer's purchase of insurance/annuity
   from depository institution or affiliates, or customer's agreement not to obtain insurance
   from unaffiliated entity

These disclosures must be made BOTH ORALLY AND IN WRITING before completion of sale.


=== ALLEGED BENEFITS OF FINANCIAL-SERVICES DIVERSIFICATION ===

CONVERGENCE: when two or more different industry types merge with each other.

Potential benefits:
- Low correlation between cash flows/revenues from traditional vs non-traditional products
- Revenue streams from different lines may move in different directions at different times
- Combining industries/products may STABILIZE combined cash flows and profitability
- Risk of failure may be reduced

This is the PRODUCT-LINE DIVERSIFICATION EFFECT.


=== DIVERSIFICATION EXAMPLE ===

Banking firm adds insurance services to its existing menu:
- Banking products: 12% average return
- Insurance services: 20% average return
- Both equally risky: 5% standard deviation each
- Revenue mix: 80% banking, 20% insurance
- Cash flow correlation: −0.50 (negatively correlated)

Result: Offering both traditional and non-traditional services
LOWERS the bank's standard deviation of overall return.


=== ECONOMIES OF SCOPE ===

Definition:
A situation where joint costs of producing two or more services in ONE FIRM
are LESS than combined cost of producing each separately.

Example formula:
If a single firm produces two services (S1 and S2) instead of only one,
using the same resources, its cost of production (C) may be lower:
  C(S1, S2) < C(S1) + C(S2)

Result: Expanding services may lead to more intensive resource use,
reducing overall costs and widening multiservice firm's profit margin.


=== INFORMATION FLOWS AND CUSTOMER PRIVACY ===

- Financial firms have become more like pure information-gathering, processing,
  and dispersing businesses

GRAMM-LEACH-BLILEY ACT of 1999:
- Allowed financial-service companies to share customer information among
  affiliated firms AND with independently owned third parties
- BUT customers must not have expressly said "no" (or "opt out") to data distribution

Protecting customer privacy became increasingly important.

A financial firm's privacy policy must include key items and be sent to customers
at least ONCE A YEAR.
