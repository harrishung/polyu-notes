=== INTRODUCTION ===
- Principal reason many financial firms are issued charters is to MAKE LOANS
- Lenders expected to supply credit for legitimate business and consumer needs at reasonable prices
- Loans support growth of new businesses and jobs in lender's market area
- Loans convey information to marketplace about a borrower's credit quality
- The lending process bears careful monitoring at all times


=== TYPES OF LOANS ===

Categories:
1. Individuals (Consumer) Loans
2. Real Estate Loans
3. Financial Institution Loans
4. Agriculture Loans
5. Commercial and Industrial (C&I) Loans
6. Miscellaneous Loans
7. Lease Financing Receivables

Largest category by dollar volume: Real Estate Loans, followed by Loans to Individuals, then C&I Loans.


=== CONSUMER LOANS (Personal, Auto, Credit Card) ===

Non-revolving loans:
- Automobile, mobile home, personal loans

Revolving loans:
- Credit card debt (Visa, MasterCard)
- Proprietary cards (Sears, AT&T)

Risks affected by competitive conditions and usury ceilings.
Bankruptcy Reform Act of 2005.

High default rates during financial crisis highlighted importance of risk evaluation
prior to making credit decisions.


=== RETAIL CREDIT DECISIONS ===
- Usually a simple accept/reject decision rather than rate adjustments
- Credit rationing
- If accepted, customers sorted by loan quantity
- For mortgages: discrimination occurs via loan-to-value rather than adjusting rates


=== FACTORS DETERMINING THE GROWTH AND MIX OF LOANS ===

1. CHARACTERISTICS OF THE MARKET AREA
   - Economic Conditions: employment, income levels, business activity
   - Demographics: population size, age, income → drives mortgage vs personal loan demand
   - Industry Presence: drives loan type demand (commercial in industrial areas)
   Examples:
   - USA: Silicon Valley tech industry drives business loan demand for startups
   - Hong Kong: Financial hub status leads to high corporate loan and trade financing demand

2. LENDER SIZE
   - Resource Availability: larger lenders → more diverse products, larger amounts
   - Risk Appetite: smaller lenders may focus on niche markets
   - Wholesale lenders vs retail credit

3. EXPERIENCE AND EXPERTISE OF MANAGEMENT

4. LOAN POLICY
   Examples:
   - USA: 2008 financial crisis — banks tightened policies, reducing subprime mortgages
   - Hong Kong: HKMA sets guidelines on mortgage lending (loan-to-value ratios)

5. EXPECTED YIELD OF EACH TYPE OF LOAN
   - Profitability: prioritize higher-yield loans for max returns
   - Risk-Return Balance
   Examples:
   - USA: Credit card loans have high yields → Citibank aggressively markets them
   - Hong Kong: High-yield personal loans popular with money lenders (UA Finance, Promise HK)

6. REGULATION
   - Compliance Requirements
   - Capital Requirements
   - Consumer Protection Laws
   Examples:
   - USA: Dodd-Frank Act introduced stricter mortgage lending regulations
   - Hong Kong: HKMA property lending regulations to prevent housing bubbles

General rule: A lending institution should make the types of loans for which it
is the most efficient producer.


=== REGULATION OF LENDING ===

Key examples of US lending regulations:
- Total real estate loans by US national bank cannot exceed bank's capital and surplus
  OR 70% of total time and savings deposits, whichever is greater
- Unsecured loan to single customer normally cannot exceed 15% of single national bank's
  unimpaired capital and surplus account (LEGAL LENDING LIMIT)

All loans subject to examination and review.


=== KEY LENDING REGULATIONS ===

COMMUNITY REINVESTMENT ACT (CRA) of 1977:
- Selected lenders must make "an affirmative effort" to meet credit needs of individuals
  and businesses in their trade territories
- No areas of local community discriminated against in seeking credit access

EQUAL CREDIT OPPORTUNITY ACT of 1974:
- No individual denied credit because of race, sex, religious affiliation, age,
  or receipt of public assistance

INTERNATIONAL LENDING AND SUPERVISION ACT:
- Requires US banks to publicly disclose credit exposures to a single country exceeding
  15% of primary capital OR 0.75% of total assets, whichever is smaller
- Imposes restrictions on fees lenders may charge troubled international borrowers
  to restructure a loan


=== UNIFORM FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS RATING SYSTEM (CAMELS) ===

Each banking firm assigned a numerical rating based on quality of asset portfolio.
Composite rating from 1 (Best) to 5 (Worst), based on six critical components.
Ratings NEVER disclosed to public to prevent bank runs.

| Letter | Component | Question |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| C | Capital Adequacy | Enough cushion to absorb losses? (Tier 1, CET1 Ratios) |
| A | Asset Quality | How risky are loans? Reserves sufficient? (NPLs, Charge-offs) |
| M | Management | Does leadership understand risks? Effective controls? (Governance) |
| E | Earnings | Profits sustainable and sufficient to grow capital? (ROA, ROE, NIM) |
| L | Liquidity | Can bank meet withdrawals without fire sales? (Funding sources) |
| S | Sensitivity | How does market risk affect earnings/capital? (Duration gap) |

Scale:
1 = Strong
2 = Satisfactory
3 = Fair (Needs Improvement)
4 = Marginal (Unsafe)
5 = Critical (Failure Imminent)

=== ESTABLISHING A GOOD WRITTEN LOAN POLICY ===

What should it contain?
1. Goal statement for the entire loan portfolio
2. Specification of lending authority of each loan officer and loan committee
3. Lines of responsibility in making assignments and reporting information
4. Operating procedures for soliciting, evaluating, and making loan decisions
5. Required documentation for all loans
6. Lines of authority for maintaining and reviewing credit files
7. Guidelines for taking, evaluating, and perfecting loan collateral
8. Procedures for setting loan rates and fees and terms for repayment
9. Statement of quality standards applicable to all loans
10. Statement of preferred upper limit for total loans outstanding
11. Description of lending institution's principal trade area
12. Procedures for detecting and working out problem loan situations


=== STEPS IN THE LENDING PROCESS ===
1. Finding Prospective Loan Customers
2. Evaluating a Customer's Character and Sincerity of Purpose
3. Making Site Visits and Evaluating a Customer's Credit Record
4. Evaluating a Prospective Customer's Financial Condition
5. Assessing Possible Loan Collateral and Signing the Loan Agreement
6. Monitoring Compliance with the Loan Agreement and Other Customer Service Needs


=== LOAN ORIGINATION PROCESS STANDARDS ===

1. KNOW YOUR CUSTOMER (KYC)
   Common practice in retail business (credit card and mortgage applications)

2. LOAN SECURITY
   - Collateral acceptable for a loan should at all times be of sufficient liquidity and value
   - Bank must ensure a sufficient margin over and above the loan value

3. SUBPRIME-LENDING RESTRICTIONS
   - Assets without collateral or insufficient collateral subject to restrictions and severe limits
   - Reflects possibility of economic downturn


=== CREDIT ANALYSIS — THE 6 Cs OF CREDIT ===

Is the Borrower Creditworthy?

a. CHARACTER: specific purpose of loan and serious intent to repay
b. CAPACITY: legal authority to sign a binding contract
c. CASH: ability to generate enough cash to repay the loan
d. COLLATERAL: adequate assets to support the loan
e. CONDITIONS: economic conditions faced by the borrower
f. CONTROL: does the loan meet the written loan policy, and how would changing
   laws/regulations affect it?


=== COMMON FINANCIAL COVENANTS ===

a. DEBT SERVICE COVERAGE RATIO (DSR / DSCR)
   - Ratio of income less expenses to interest and principal payments
   - Main measure to determine if cash flow is sufficient to service debt

b. LOAN LIFE COVERAGE RATIO (LLCR)
   - Net present value of cash flow available for debt servicing to total debt
   - Like DSCR but useful in project finance — analysis covers the term of the transaction
   - More difficult to monitor if project cash flows are inconsistent

c. LOAN TO VALUE RATIO (LTV)
   - Central to residential mortgage lending
   - Allows banks to gauge how far prices can fall before loss is incurred
     should they foreclose and liquidate the property


=== STRUCTURING THE LOAN AGREEMENT ===

Can the Loan Agreement Be Properly Structured and Documented?
- Draft a loan agreement meeting borrower's funding need with comfortable repayment schedule
- If a major borrower gets into trouble, lender may be in trouble too
- Proper accommodation may involve lending more or less than requested over longer/shorter period


=== PERFECTING THE CLAIM ===

Can the Lender Perfect Its Claim against the Borrower's Earnings and Pledged Collateral?

Reasons for Taking Collateral:
- If borrower cannot pay, pledge gives lender right to seize and sell those assets
- Provides psychological advantage over the borrower

Types of Collateral:
- Accounts Receivables
- Factoring
- Inventory
- Real Property
- Personal Property
- Personal Guarantees


=== CREDIT ANALYSIS — INTERNAL RATINGS ===

Ratings act as basis for bank credit approval, pricing, monitoring, and loan loss provisioning.

Two dimensions of ratings:
1. Borrower propensity to default
2. Transaction characteristics (products, terms, seniority, collateral)

BASEL I:
- Standardized Approach: banks use ratings developed by external rating agencies
- Loan pricing model based on capital required and target return on that capital

BASEL II:
- Banks meeting strict criteria permitted to use internal ratings to calculate
  regulatory capital for credit risk
- Internal ratings can be more sensitive to risk in bank's portfolio
- May incorporate supplementary customer information not available to external agencies
- Banks have incentive to refine internal credit risk management/measurement
- Credit ratings used to determine risk weightings under Standardized Approach

=== LOAN REVIEW ===

1. Carry out reviews of all types of loans on a periodic basis
2. Structure the loan review process:
   - Record of borrower payments
   - Quality and condition of collateral
   - Completeness of loan documentation
   - Evaluation of borrower's financial condition
   - Assessment as to whether loan fits with lender's loan policies
3. Review largest loans most frequently
4. Conduct more frequent reviews of troubled loans
5. Accelerate the loan review schedule if economy or industry experiences problems


=== LOAN WORKOUTS ===

Loan workout = the process of recovering funds from a problem loan situation.


=== WARNING SIGNS OF PROBLEM LOANS ===
a. Unusual or unexpected delays in receiving financial statements
b. Sudden changes in accounting methods
c. Restructuring debt or eliminating dividend payments / changes in credit rating
d. Adverse changes in stock price
e. Losses in one or more years
f. Adverse changes in capital structure
g. Deviations in actual sales from projections
h. Unexpected or unexplained changes in deposits


=== INDICATORS OF WEAK/TROUBLED LOANS (FDIC Manual) ===
- Irregular or delinquent loan payments
- Frequent alterations in loan terms
- Poor loan renewal record (little reduction of principal at renewal)
- Unusually high loan rate (compensating for high risk)
- Unusual buildup of borrower's accounts receivable and/or inventories
- Rising debt-to-net-worth (leverage) ratio
- Missing documentation (especially financial statements)
- Poor-quality collateral
- Reliance on reappraisals of assets to increase customer's net worth
- Absence of cash flow statements or projections
- Customer reliance on non-recurring sources of funds to meet loan payments
  (e.g., selling buildings or equipment)


=== INDICATORS OF INADEQUATE/POOR LENDING POLICIES ===
- Poor selection of risks among borrowing customers
- Lending money contingent on possible future events (e.g., a merger)
- Lending money because customer promises a large deposit
- Failure to specify a plan for loan liquidation
- High proportion of loans outside the lender's trade territory
- Incomplete credit files
- Substantial self-dealing credits (loans to insiders — employees, directors, stockholders)
- Tendency to overreact to competition (poor loans to keep customers from competitors)
- Lending money to support speculative purchases
- Lack of sensitivity to changing economic conditions


=== STEPS WHEN A LOAN IS IN TROUBLE ===
1. Don't forget the goal: MAXIMIZE FULL RECOVERY OF FUNDS
2. Rapid detection and reporting of problems is essential
3. Loan workout should be SEPARATE from the lending function
4. Consult with the customer quickly regarding possible options
5. Estimate resources available to collect on loan
6. Conduct tax and litigation search
7. Evaluate the quality and competence of management
8. Consider all reasonable alternatives

PREFERRED OPTION: Seek a revised loan agreement.


=== CREDIT DERIVATIVES — RISKS ===

Contracts for Reducing Credit Risk Exposure on the Balance Sheet.

Risks Associated with Credit Derivatives:
1. Partners in swap or option contract may fail to perform
2. Smaller volume — markets are thinner and more volatile
3. Legal issues
4. Regulatory concerns
5. Lessons of recent Credit Crisis:
   - Securitized assets and credit swaps are complex instruments — difficult to value
     and measure risk exposures correctly
   - These derivatives operate in cyclically sensitive markets
   - Contagion effect cannot be stopped without active government intervention
